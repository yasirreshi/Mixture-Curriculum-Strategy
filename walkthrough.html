Act I — the trap

Why mixture plans go wrong, and the one rule that stops it

Every share is taken from another share. The failure mode is not arithmetic — it is assigning a confident percentage to a capability that has almost no data behind it, then never checking.

A mixture looks like a budgeting exercise and is really a supply problem. The lanes this model is for — agentic work, controllable reasoning, native Indic — are precisely the lanes with the least data in existence. The lane that is easiest to fill, general web, is the one nobody brags about. So the plan needed a rule that makes an unfundable share impossible to write down rather than merely discouraged.

The governing invariant

No lane may exceed 4 epochs over its unique supply. Muennighoff et al. (arXiv:2305.16264) measured the cost of repetition: up to four passes is near-indistinguishable from fresh data at fixed compute, and past that the value of added compute decays toward zero.

share × 3.0T ≤ 4 × ( collected unique tokens + tokens we have costed a way to manufacture )

The rule is applied asymmetrically, on purpose: when a lane fails the test the share is cut, and the cap is never raised. That asymmetry is the only thing separating this document from a spreadsheet of round numbers — and it is enforced by a script that exits non-zero, not by good intentions.

Two consequences run through everything below. First, supply is quoted as unique tokens, never dataset size — a 900B-token corpus used twice is not 1.8T of supply. Second, where a share can only be reached by manufacturing data, the plan says so in the same breath as the number, and costs the manufacturing separately.

The agentic ledger — the number to attack first

Unique agentic supply is 0.63B tokens, two orders of magnitude below every other lane. The session's composer sets a floor of Agentic ≥ 2%; this plan runs 0.96%, under it. That is a deliberate breach, and the composer's own supply check is the argument for it: at its 2T run, 2% is 40B of demand against 0.63B of real supply — 63 epochs. The floor as printed cannot be met by collected data at any run size the session considers. It is a synthesis commitment, so the honest question is not 2% or 0.96% but how much can actually be manufactured.

collected unique agentic supply 0.63B + synthesis programme, costed per environment family 10.00B ─────── unique supply 10.63B × 4-epoch cap max 42.50B spent: main run 27.9B + anneal 10.0B 37.90B → 3.57 epochs ✓ the session composer's 2% would spend 68.00B → 6.40 epochs ✗

The 10B is costed per environment family, because the binding resource is verified environments, not generation FLOPs: repo tasks from permissive repos with CI (300k × 9k = 2.7B), terminal sandbox tasks (250k × 4k = 1.0B), API calls against mock services generated from OpenAPI specs (600k × 3k = 1.8B), browse tasks over a cached snapshot (150k × 12k = 1.8B), and multi-turn user simulation including Hindi and Hinglish (400k × 6k = 2.4B). A trajectory is kept only if the environment confirms the outcome the trace claims.

The conclusion that makes 0.96% defensible

Agentic capability is not bought in pretraining. Pretraining installs the format — action/observation alternation, tool-call syntax, failure-and-recovery structure. SWE-bench is won in SFT and RLVR, where 10–20B tokens is a large budget rather than a rounding error. A 2% pretraining share would be paying six epochs for a format lesson.

And the masking convention that lane forces into the spec

Loss falls on the model's own planning, tool calls and final answer. Tool observations, repo files and test output are context and carry no token loss; the verifier's pass/fail is reward-only. Training on observations teaches the model to invent tool results instead of calling the tool. This is why the cleaning pass stores a mask per segment rather than a blob of text — and why it could measure what that mask costs (Act III).

The Indic slot, priced tier by tier

A single headline number would hide the only constraint that matters here. The session preset is 40/25/20/15; 40% verified is 208B of main-run demand against ~85B of verified supply even after the collection programme — it does not fit. So the main run takes 28% verified and the anneal runs 70% verified: the best Indic tokens are spent at low learning rate on a model that can use them, which is the entire argument for having a reserve at all.

Which languages, and the Sanskrit rule

All twelve plus Hinglish enter at stage A, not later: script and morphology must be learned while the embedding table is still plastic, and V4's gradient incident came precisely from raising an Indic share late against settled embeddings. Hinglish is a first-class member, not a contaminant — it is how a large fraction of Indian users actually type. Urdu's share is protected against being absorbed into Hindi on the argument that the two "sound similar".

Sanskrit gets 0% of the main run, and the rule is general: a language earns a funded pretraining slice at ≥1B unique verified tokens. Below that, a share only buys repetition of a small corpus — memorisation, not competence. Sanskrit instead gets representation in the tokenizer's balanced sample, 0.2B inside the anneal reserve for classical grounding where the model can generalise from little data, and a place in evaluation. Maithili, Konkani, Dogri, Santali and the rest are collection and evaluation targets now, pretraining lanes when the threshold is met.

Why the Indic lane's real cost is measured in tokens, not words

Fertility on the 32k proxy tokenizer built in this repo — tokens consumed per whitespace word. An Indic word costs two to four times an English one, which is the tax the 200k production vocab exists to pay down, and the reason a "17.9% Indic share" buys less language than it sounds like.

Act III — what the corpus said

Four findings from cleaning real data

Two constraints shaped the method before any of this ran. No GPU, so the 1B/3B protocol is written for a cluster and what executed here is the same instrument three orders of magnitude down. And Hugging Face was unreachable from this network — every source re-routed to Wikimedia dumps, raw GitHub, Gutenberg and arXiv. That second constraint turned out to matter: pulling PRM800K as a raw file rather than a processed dataset is what later made the L3 reconstruction possible at all.

Overturned #1 — how the agentic lane is sized

Of 11,781,899 agentic tokens, only 4,529,722 carry loss. The lane is 38.4% supervised; the rest is tool schemas, user turns and observation slots the model reads but must never reproduce. Sizing from dataset bytes overstates the learning signal by 2.6×. The plan now states shares in consumed tokens — what the run pays for in compute — and tracks supervised tokens as a separate quantity.

Overturned #2 — "85B of reasoning supply" is a gross number

At verified-step quality it is a fraction of that, and the shape is worse than the volume: the entire reachable open corpus is band L1/L2. Zero traces reach L3 (≥1024 reasoning tokens). The long half of the effort dial does not exist and must be distilled — costed at 25B with a delivery gate. The obvious objection (ceiling 992 against an L3 line at 1024 looks engineered) is answered by the distribution, not the threshold: the mass sits an order of magnitude below the boundary.

Lanes fed by curated sources fill trivially; a public-domain book simply is long-context reserve material. The two lanes this model exists for do not fill at all. At a 0.6% fill rate a 10B agentic reserve would need ~1.55T of cleaned agentic candidates — and total real supply is 0.63B. 05
Build the band that does not exist09_build_long_traces.py

PRM800K is not a list of solutions — it is a tree search over them, in which humans rated 60,398 completions as wrong. Step 02 discarded all of that to get clean linear traces. Replaying the search in the order it happened — rejected attempt, correction marker, then the continuation actually chosen — is the plan's own recipe ("keep the failed branches that were later corrected") executed rather than promised. Every token is original generator output and every verdict a human's; only the ordering is constructed, and it ships in its own D_constructed shard.

Act IV — what the experiments said

Three runs, and the plan losing two of them

The pre-registered protocol is 8 arms × 1B params × 30B tokens, then 3B × 60B — 4.68e21 FLOPs, 0.65% of the run it protects. What ran here is the same instrument at 11.4M parameters and 3.0M tokens per arm: enough to find direction and to catch flaws in the protocol itself, not enough to confirm a share.

Held — the pre-registered refutation did not fire

The kill condition was web-heavy lands within CI of the plan. It came last by 16%: it buys 3.5% better web loss and pays +10.8% code, +11.1% Indic, +16.3% reasoning, +37% agentic. Per-lane spread between best and worst arm — agentic 46.2%, reasoning 17.6%, Indic 17.0%, code 14.6% — says the same thing. The mixture is a capability decision, measurably.

Overturned #3 — the experiment could not answer its own question

Three arms beat the plan, and two of them win by overspending scarce lanes. But this ablation samples every lane with replacement, so a bigger share always helps and no arm ever pays for repetition — which is the entire basis of the agentic number. The instrument was blind to the one thing it was aimed at. That became a required correction to the 1B protocol and the next run.

Confirmed — one artifact diagnosed correctly

indic_heavy's wins over the plan on reasoning (−1.14% → +0.18%), agentic (−1.88% → +0.29%) and STEM (−0.43% → +0.33%) all flip to losses once repetition is charged for, and its Indic win halves. Its earlier lead was the instrument, exactly as diagnosed.

Overturned #4 — the agentic share, and it was the one that mattered

v5_agentic_2pct still beats the plan on the agentic lane by 5.7% while repeating an 11k-token pool 5.5 times against the plan's 2.7 — and takes the best W. The 4-epoch cap is a cliff in the arithmetic and a slope in measurement. One validity objection survives, and it is specific: the agentic hold-out cannot separate learned tool use from memorising the Gorilla/BFCL format. So 0.96% is now explicitly provisional, the 1B protocol gains an out-of-distribution hold-out to settle it, and a written rule raises the share to 2.0% together with the synthesis target, 10B → 17B, if it survives that test. The share never moves without the supply that funds it.

Overturned #5 — the protected floor

Zero of 90 Indic candidate batches survive selection. The selector does not under-value Indic; it erases it. A 12% absolute floor would have delivered exactly 12% against a 17.9% schedule — a 5.9-point shortfall in every batch across 2.9T tokens. So the floor cannot be an absolute number below the schedule; it has to be a fraction of it. Indic 12% + agentic 0.5% + long reasoning 2% = 14.5% → 90% of each protected lane's scheduled share = 23.3% of every batch. The cost is stated rather than hidden: OPUS's efficiency gain now applies to ~77% of each batch against ~92% under V4's 8% floor.

Overturned #6 — the 500-token prefix is not a detail

Long-context candidates retain 0.0% scored on a document's opening and 77.8% scored anywhere else in the same documents — a 78-point swing from scorer placement alone. The session makes this point about agentic traces; it generalises to every lane whose payload is not in the opening lines.

Overturned #7 — the burden of proof on general web

Web is the flattest of the large lanes, the cheapest to cut in loss terms — which argues against the plan's own case for holding it at 31%. Loss on web is not downstream MMLU, so this is not a refutation, but the burden of proof now sits with me and §2 says so. Separately, the long-context row is the instrument failing: at context 256 a model cannot express long-context capability at all, so the loss barely responds to share. That became the second correction to the 1B protocol — stage-1 arms must train at a context where the lane means something, or they will report "long context does not matter" and be believed.

What held under test

  • The 4-epoch cap as a design rule. It failed the agentic lane at 2% and passed it at 0.96%; the share was cut and the cap was not raised.
  • The mixture beats naive web-heavy decisively — the one pre-registered kill condition, and it did not fire.
  • The reserve is a manufacturing problem, not a selection problem — 0.6% and 0.0% fill on the two lanes that matter.
  • The Indic tier deviation. 40% verified does not fit the supply at any run size considered; 28% in the main run and 70% in the anneal does.

What is still unsettled

  • The agentic share. 0.96% survives on a validity objection, not on a win. The 1B run with an OOD hold-out settles it, and the rule for either outcome is already written.
  • General web at 31%. The elasticity fit argues against it; held on the grounds that held-out loss is not a downstream benchmark.
  • L4 traces. Do not exist in reachable open data and cannot be reconstructed from PRM800K's search depth. Until the 25B distillation lands, the top of the effort dial is unfunded.
  • Everything at scale. Eleven models of 11.4M parameters cannot confirm shares for a 40B run. They were run to find direction and to catch the two protocol flaws above — which they did, for 2.6 CPU-hours.

Three ordering decisions worth defending

Indic starts at 15%, above its floor, in stage A. Script and morphology have to be learned while the embedding table is still plastic. V4's incident is the evidence: a step change in the Hindi share met frozen embeddings and the gradient norm jumped ~150×. Every transition here is blended over an 8B-token warm-up, with a rollback trigger at >3× over a 2B-token window.

Long context arrives only in stage C, after the model can already read and reason, stepping 8K→16K→32K inside the stage with length-homogeneous batches.

Indic peaks at 24% in stage D, not stage A, because cross-lingual transfer is most efficient from an already-strong technical model — and web sits at its floor by then.

Two ladders, scheduled independently

Difficulty runs the session's six rungs, B0 nursery to B5 research/PhD, moving 25/35/25/12/3/0 in stage A to 0/5/15/25/35/20 in stage D. Trace length runs L0 direct to L4 ultra and is scheduled separately — a hard problem with a short trace and an easy one with an ultra trace are not interchangeable, so both are stamped per document and both move per stage. Every rung is anchored to a real document in the cleaned corpus with its measured token count.
